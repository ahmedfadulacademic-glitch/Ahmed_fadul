Selecting the best site for a new city

Where in Meroe Locality, Northern State, Sudan, could a new city be built sustainably? I answered it with GIS, remote sensing and a weighted suitability model.

TypeB.Sc. graduation project
UniversityUniversity of Khartoum, Faculty of Engineering, Surveying Engineering
TeamAhmed Fadul and Ahmed Abdelrahman Alnour Yousif
SupervisorDr. Awad Aljeed Mohammed Awad Aljeed

The problem

Choosing where to build a new city affects water supply, flood risk, cost and growth for decades. The aim was to propose suitable locations in Meroe Locality using a repeatable, evidence-based method instead of judgement alone.

Project workflow

  1. Collect open dataEverything came from public sources, at resolutions from a few metres to tens of kilometres.
    Elevation DEM · USGSRoads, rivers, cities · OpenStreetMapTemperature, rainfall · WorldClimSoil texture · OpenLandMapGroundwater · BGSLand use · Esri 10 m
  3. Prepare the layersReprojected every layer to WGS84 / UTM Zone 36, clipped it to the Meroe Locality boundary, and merged layers where coverage was split.
  5. Derive new layersCalculated slope from the DEM, then ran a hydrological analysis to map wadis and stream order for the flood and flash-flood layers.
  7. Standardise the criteriaRescored each of the 16 criteria to one common suitability scale so they can be compared.
  9. Weight with AHPThe Analytic Hierarchy Process compares criteria in pairs to decide how much each one should count.
  11. Weighted overlayCombined all weighted layers in ArcGIS into one suitability surface.
  13. ClassifyGrouped the surface into suitability classes: high, moderate and low (no area reached “ideal”).
  15. DeliverSuitability map, area statistics for each class, and the road corridors where the high-suitability land concentrates: Karima–Dongola and Merowe–Atbara.

16 criteria

TerrainElevation, slope
Water and hazardsProximity to rivers, groundwater, flood-prone areas, flash-flood areas
Land and climateSoil type, temperature, rainfall, land use, proximity to agricultural land
Access and infrastructureRoads, railways, power lines, major cities, airports

Criteria layers

Three of the 16 prepared layers, as examples of what went into the model.

Results

High suitability774.54 km²4% of the locality
Moderate suitability14,864 km²85% of the locality
Low suitability1,972 km²11% of the locality

No area reached the “ideal” class. The study concentrates the high-suitability land along the Karima–Dongola and Merowe–Atbara road corridors, which is where growth and services are most needed.

Limits and next steps

The model is a screening tool: it narrows a very large area to the places that deserve a closer look. The report recommends field studies of the high-suitability areas, adding social and population-density criteria, building a national spatial database, and testing deep learning for suitability modelling.
